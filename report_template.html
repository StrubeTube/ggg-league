The Fire-Sale Fix

GGG

Every year, teams that fall out of the race sell real players to contenders for future picks — players worth nothing to the seller, everything to the buyer. This proposal adds one number per player and one rule, then replays our actual 2024 and 2025 seasons to show exactly which trades it would have stopped. Nothing moves off Sleeper.

The receipts

We don't have a trading problem. We have a pricing problem.

A rebuilding team's studs cost them nothing to give away — they're only keeping three players anyway. So contenders buy stars for picks alone. It's rational, it's legal, and it decides our playoffs. From our own transaction log:

Amendment A · The Cap Keeper Rule

Every player gets a price. Every trade has to fit.

No auction, no contracts, no spreadsheets. The draft we already run sets every salary automatically.

A1Salary by draft round. Every player's salary is fixed by the round they were drafted in (table below). Keepers count at their escalated round — exactly like today. Waiver and free-agent pickups cost $1. Draft picks carry $0.
A2$200 team cap. Your roster's total salary can never exceed $200 after a trade. A normal drafted roster starts around $170–$180, so the cap only bites when someone tries to stack stars.
A3Trades must fit — both sides. If a deal puts either roster over $200, it can't process as constructed. Send real salary back, or it doesn't happen. The commissioner reverses violations; the cap sheet is public to everyone.
A4Nothing else changes. Same keepers, same escalation, same waivers, same deadlines. Pre-draft keeper-rights trades stay exempt — the cap sheet is born on draft night.
Full disclosure

What the cap alone doesn't catch

The cap blocked the mega-buys, but two smaller fire sales stayed legal because the buyer had room (Kenneth Walker to a 4-0 team in 2025 for one bench back and a 5th). And today's rules still reward selling: draft order is the inverse of playoff finish, so gutting your roster improves your pick. Two companion amendments close the loop:

Amendment B

Draft order by points-for

Non-playoff teams draft in order of total points scored, not inverse finish. Sell your studs and your roster stops scoring — and your draft slot sinks with it. Tanking stops paying twice.

Amendment C

The rental tax

A player acquired by in-season trade costs one round higher to keep. Buyers stop getting this year's production and a discounted keeper in the same deal — rentals become rentals.
