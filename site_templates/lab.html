The rule simulator

Every slider recomputes all ten teams instantly, using the keeper selections saved on this device. Defaults are the minimal fire-sale fix — salaries, a cap, and a floor on top of the keeper league you already know. The default lines ($230 cap / $150 floor) are fitted to history: the pair that blocks every tagged fire sale since 2020 while blocking the fewest normal trades. Gold boxes mark what's net new versus the old rules — flip a control back to the old way and its box disappears. Settings save locally.

The sliders

Salary table
Flatness
Flatter → more stars kept, league drifts toward dynasty. Steeper → stars price out fast, more turnover, all keeper value hides in the late rounds.
Team cap
Lower → tighter market: fire-sale hauls impossible to absorb, more cap jail, fewer blockbusters. Higher → looser market: easier star-stacking, deadline arms races return.
Team floor
Higher → fire sales die: sellers must take real salary back ("field a real team"). Lower/off → liquidations return. Too high and rebuilders can't sell at all. Waivers never trip it.
Keeper budget
The master redraft↔dynasty dial. Higher → dynasty: better players kept, thinner draft, slower turnover. Lower → redraft: harsh keep-or-release choices, richer draft night.
Max keepers
Higher → rebuilders hold more cheap lottery tickets. Lower → more late-round talent returns to the draft. The budget, not this, governs real value.
Keeper slotting
Off the board → keeping stars gets easier (costs dollars + a tail pick, draft stays clean). Round-slot → keeping stars gets brutal (costs your actual early pick; the old way).
Budget mode — pairs with the next control: together they set the true cost of keeping
Decides which keeps must fit the keeper budget. 3-guaranteed (old way) → your top three are always free of the budget; only extras need it. Budget-governs-all → every keep is a budget decision and stars circulate more.
Keeper salaries count against the team cap — the other half of the pair
Decides whether keeper salaries also consume your season cap. Count (old-style accounting) → good keepers cost season flexibility, and with 3-guaranteed the cap is what stops stud hoarding. Exempt → keeping is never punished, but then the budget above must do all the limiting.
Franchise keep (budget-exempt star)
On → star retention up, fire-sale pressure down (everyone always keeps their one guy; bounded to a single player). Off → tighter keeping, more stars back in the draft.
Apply these rules to the Home page
While on, the Home page keeper corner and cap board use these Lab settings instead of the real rules, with a banner so nobody mistakes the sandbox for policy. Per device.
